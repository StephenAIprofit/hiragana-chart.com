Hiragana dakuten and handakuten

Dakuten add two dots (が ざ だ ば). Handakuten add a circle (ぱ ぴ ぷ ぺ ぽ). In modern Japanese, ぢ is read ji and づ is read zu.

Hiragana yoon (contracted sounds)

Yoon is a large kana plus a small ゃ, ゅ, or ょ. The small character is not an independent sound. ぢゃ, ぢゅ, and ぢょ are rare; they use the same sound as じゃ, じゅ, and じょ.

Common questions

How do I read a Hiragana chart?

Read left to right, top to bottom. Each row shares a consonant: the ka row is ka, ki, ku, ke, ko. Learn by row, not by column. The empty cells in the ya row and wa row are sounds that do not exist in modern Japanese. Try it: pick the a row, click each character, and say it aloud while you look at its stroke order.

How many characters are there, including Dakuten and Yoon?

There are 46 basic characters, from あ to ん. Dakuten and Handakuten add 25, for 71 in total. Yoon adds 36, for 107 in total. The obsolete ゐ and ゑ are not included. Dakuten, Handakuten, and Yoon are not a separate alphabet; they are extensions of the same 46 sounds.

What Romaji does this chart use?

Hepburn Romaji. し is shi, not si; ち is chi, not ti; つ is tsu, not tu; ふ is fu; を is written wo. This matters for typing: type じ as ji and ぢ as di, even though both sound like ji in modern Japanese. The same applies to ず as zu and づ as du.

How do I pronounce shi, chi, tsu, fu, and ん?

し is shi, ち is chi, つ is tsu, like the end of cats, ふ is a soft fu blown between the lips, halfway between h and f. ん is a syllabic n and takes one beat: にほん has three beats, ni-ho-n. Click any character on the chart to hear it, then say it aloud while you write it.

Why do は, へ, and を sound different in sentences?

On the chart you see their dictionary readings: ha, he, wo. In sentences they are particles and change sound: は is wa, へ is e, and を is o. Example: は marks the topic, へ marks direction, and を marks the object. Do not relearn the characters; learn the particle use on top of the chart reading.

What is the difference between Hiragana and Katakana, and when do I use each?

Hiragana writes native Japanese words and grammar. Katakana writes foreign loanwords, names, and emphasis. The sounds are the same 46 sounds. Example: コーヒー and コンピューター are Katakana because they are loanwords. Use the Hiragana / Katakana switcher on the chart to see the same table in the other script, then test yourself in the Quiz in Katakana mode.

What are Dakuten, Handakuten, and Yoon?

Dakuten adds two dots and voices the sound: か becomes が. Handakuten adds a circle: は becomes ぱ. Yoon is a large kana plus a small ゃ, ゅ, or ょ, and counts as one beat, not two: きゃ is kya, one beat, while きや is ki-ya, two beats. In modern Japanese, ぢゃ, ぢゅ, and ぢょ sound the same as じゃ, じゅ, and じょ, and are rare.

What are Sokuon and long vowels?

Sokuon is a small っ or ッ. It has no sound of its own; it doubles the next consonant and takes one beat. In Romaji this shows as a doubled letter: がっこう is gakkou, with kk. For long vowels, Katakana uses ー, as in コーヒー. Hiragana uses おう, おお, or えい, as in おかあさん, とおい, and せんせい. Tap an example to hear the doubled consonant.

Do I need to learn stroke order, and how do I see it?

Click any character. The panel shows its numbered strokes in order. For Yoon, it shows the base character first, then the small kana, and the stroke count is the sum. If you only need to read, you can skip stroke order. If you write by hand, correct stroke order keeps characters balanced and readable. Practice by tracing the Printable chart, then writing the same character without looking.

How long does it take to learn Hiragana, and how should I practice?

Most learners recognize the basic 46 in one to two weeks at one row a day, then need more weeks to read them quickly. That is a typical range, not a guarantee. Practice in this order: learn one row a day in gojuon order, say each character aloud while you write it, test yourself backwards and shuffled, then take Quiz L1. When L1 is comfortable, move to L2 for Dakuten and Handakuten, then L3 for Yoon.
