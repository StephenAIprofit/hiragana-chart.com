Translator questions

How do I look up a Kanji by drawing it?

Write one Kanji at a time in the draw box. Draw in the usual stroke order if you can; it helps recognition. If the result is wrong, try clearer, separated strokes, or switch to typing instead. The draw box is for one character, not for a whole word or sentence.

How do I look up a Kanji by typing?

You can type English, Romaji, or kana. English matches the meaning. Romaji is converted to Hiragana first, so use Hepburn spelling: shi, not si, and chi, not ti. Kana matches the reading. If you type a reading, you will get Kanji that share that reading, not just one result.

What does a result show?

Each result card shows three lines: the Kanji in large type on the first line, its reading on the second line, and its English meaning on the third line, up to two lines. The card also shows stroke order where available. Use the reading to check pronunciation, and the meaning to check whether it is the Kanji you meant.

Why do I get more than one result?

Because one reading can belong to many Kanji, and one English word can match many meanings. Exact English matches appear first. If the first result is not the one you want, check the reading and meaning on each card before you pick one, rather than picking the first large character.

What should I do if I get no result?

First check the input: in draw mode, make sure it is one character; in type mode, check Hepburn spelling and try the kana reading instead of English, or English instead of Romaji. If you typed a whole phrase, try a single word first. If you still get no result, the word may not be in the lookup data; try a simpler synonym.

How does the Translator connect to the Chart and Quiz?

The Translator is for looking up Kanji, not for learning the 46 kana. If you cannot read the kana in a result, go back to the chart to review that row, then use the Quiz to practice reading it. If you already know the kana, use the Translator to move from a word you know to the Kanji you need.
