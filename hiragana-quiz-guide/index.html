Hiragana Quiz: Practice the 46, Dakuten, and Yoon

The quiz turns the chart into a test: one question at a time, instant scoring, and your score, combo, and accuracy as you go. Test the basic 46, or add dakuten and yoon for 107. The point is not to pass once — it is to find the kana you keep missing, fix them on the chart, and test yourself again.

The levels: L1, L2, L3 (and L4)

The quiz has four level buttons, but only three sets:

  • L1 Basic 46. The 46 basic hiragana, from あ to ん. Start here.
  • L2 + Dakuten. The basic 46 plus dakuten and handakuten, for 71.
  • L3 + Yoon. Everything, for 107. This is the full set.
  • L4 All. The same set as L3, not a separate set. Pick either one.

In Characters mode a round is the full set: 46 questions at L1, 71 at L2, 107 at L3. In Words mode the rounds are shorter: 32 words at L1, 52 at L2, 72 at L3. L4 matches L3 in both modes.

The quiz gives the rule for moving up: "Start at L1, and move up only when you can pass the current level without looking at the chart." Do not jump to L3 while L2 still trips you up.

Two modes: Characters and Words

Characters shows one kana or one reading and asks you to pick the match from four choices: see あ, pick "a". Use it when a level is new, or when one pair keeps getting mixed up.

Words shows a simple English word — water, moon, flower — and asks you to pick the correct kana spelling from four choices. Example: school is がっこう. There is no typing box, so Words tests recognition, not spelling. Add it once you know the 46: Words is where dakuten and yoon appear inside real words.

Question direction: only Characters mode

Question direction has two settings, and they only affect Characters mode. Romaji → Kana shows a reading — "so" — and asks "Which kana matches this reading?" Kana → Romaji shows a kana — う — and asks "Which romaji matches this character?" Both are four-choice picks, and switching resets the round to the first question. In Words mode the setting is ignored.

Script: Hiragana and Katakana

Script switches between Hiragana and Katakana. In Characters mode the heading becomes "Katakana Quiz" and the choices become katakana; the levels stay the same. In Words mode the switch does nothing: the choices stay hiragana. So practise katakana in Characters mode — Words mode is hiragana only.

What happens when you get one wrong

A wrong answer is not the end of the round. Your pick turns red, the correct one turns green, and the quiz says, for example: "Not this one. る is ru." A red "Next" button appears; the quiz does not move on by itself. A right answer turns the button green — "Correct. す is su." — and score and combo each go up by one.

Wrong answers do not come back inside the same round. In Characters mode each round asks each kana once, in a random order, so a kana you miss will not reappear until the round is over. When the round ends, the screen says "Round done" — for example "45 correct, 1 to review. Best combo: 45." — with two buttons: "Review wrong answers" and "Start again".

"Review wrong answers" starts a short separate round with only the kana you missed — progress "1 / 1", no special label. Score, combo, and accuracy carry over, and a right answer adds to them. When it ends, the screen shows only the review count — "1 correct, 0 to review. Best combo: 46." — and only "Start again" is left.

Nothing is saved to a server. Reload or close the tab and everything resets — score, combo, accuracy, progress, settings — and you start again from the first question, in a fresh random order.

How to practise with the chart

The quiz and the chart are one loop. Take a round at your current level. When you miss one, do not just press "Next" and forget it: after the round, open the chart, click that kana, listen to the audio, and watch the stroke order. Say the sound out loud, then run the review round. If the same pair keeps failing — ね and れ, シ and ツ — the confusable kana guide covers how to tell them apart.

A simple routine

Ten minutes a day is enough. Days at L1: Characters mode, Romaji → Kana, until you can pass without looking at the chart. Then L2 the same way, then L3. Add Words mode once a level feels easy. Finish every session with the review round. Once a week, switch Script to Katakana and run L1 in Characters mode again.

FAQ

Should I start at L1 even if I know some hiragana already?

Yes. L1 takes a few minutes, and it shows which kana you actually know. Start there, and move up only when you can pass without looking.

Why do wrong answers not come back in the same round?

In Characters mode each round asks each kana once, so a missed kana cannot reappear until the round ends. The review round is where the missed ones come back.

Does the quiz remember my score?

No. Wrong answers return later in the same session for review, and nothing is saved to a server. Reload or close the page and you start again, with a new random order.

Does Words mode work in katakana?

No. Switching Script to Katakana changes Characters mode only. In Words mode the choices stay hiragana. Use Characters mode to practise katakana.

Start with L1

Open the quiz and start at L1. If you want the katakana version, open the Katakana Quiz — same levels, katakana script, Characters mode. Pass a level without the chart, then move up.
